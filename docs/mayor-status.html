Gameplay

How to play

A city grows when three things are in place: a street to reach it, a zone to build on and services to live with. The R, C and I bars show what the city is asking for.

  1. Draw the streetRoads tool: line or curve, at any angle. The preview shows cost, length, slope and the reason when something cannot be built.
  2. Paint the zoneResidential, commercial or industrial, up to 4 cells from the street. Cells follow their own street, diagonal or curved.
  3. Connect servicesPower, water, sewage and garbage; then police, fire, health, education and parks. Coverage shows in the infoview layers.
  4. Let it growSet the clock to 2× or 3×. Each lot goes through construction, gets residents or jobs and evolves with land value and demand.
  5. ManageTaxes, budget, transit, traffic and employment. Alerts arrive through the bell; the starter guide explains each step.
  • 1
    Clock and funds.
    Population, jobs, date, monthly balance and speed (pause, 1×, 2×, 3×), undo and redo.
  • 2
    R, C, I demand.
    Green, blue and yellow: how much the city wants of each zone. A bar going up asks for more, going down means surplus.
  • 3
    Indicators.
    Popularity, budget, environment and traffic, each from 0 to 100.
  • 4
    Services.
    Use and capacity of power, water, sewage and garbage, and police, fire, health and education coverage.
  • 5
    Starter guide.
    One card per step, advancing on its own when you complete the step.
  • 6
    Tools.
    Move, Roads, Zones, Services, Transit, Bulldoze, Infoview and Economy.

Roads and junctions

The street is the reference for everything

Every road type has a profile: asphalt, kerb, sidewalk and setback. The width of lots, sidewalks, lanes and junctions comes from that profile, never from a constant.

Road types

RoadAsphaltSidewalkLanesNotes
Street0.380.122Serves lots on both sides
Avenue0.810.164Median, signalised
Highway1.4004No lots, no at-grade crossing
One-wayas the roadsame1 directionArrows and connectors only in the legal direction

half-width in tiles; 1 tile = 8 m

What the tool accepts and refuses

  • Any angle is accepted: nothing is rounded. 15°, 45° and 90° guides are optional.
  • Touch snaps to a node, then the centreline, with a constant 36 px radius (16 px for mouse) at every zoom.
  • A crossing or arm under 20° is refused with a reason; arms under 5° merge.
  • A road on top of a road, duplicated or 0.3 tile from another is refused, suggesting a parallel road or a road upgrade.
  • A new junction needs 1.35 tiles of distance from another.

How a junction is drawn

Each node becomes a ring of layers: asphalt, kerb, sidewalk and a safety boundary. Corners are arcs tangent to the arm edges. When the angle tightens, the radius shrinks to a 0.2 tile floor and then becomes a chamfer, always with a recorded diagnostic.

flowchart LR
  A[Node arms in angular order] --> B[Setback per arm: max of its two corners]
  B --> C{Does the full radius fit?}
  C -- yes --> D[Tangent fillet]
  C -- no --> E[Smaller radius, floor 0.2]
  E --> F{Still does not fit?}
  F -- yes --> G[Chamfer and diagnostic]
  F -- no --> D
  D --> H[Nested, simple rings]
  G --> H
  H --> I[Test: no overlap, no gap, no sliver triangle]
Zones and lots

Attached to the street, at the street's angle

Zone cells do not live on the map grid: they are born in each segment's own frame and follow the street, diagonal or curved. Where the street cannot support valid cells, painting is refused.

The rules

  • Up to 4 cells deep from the street; the lot front sits on the sidewalk plus the profile setback.
  • A cell is valid only if its physical footprint does not touch asphalt, sidewalk or the junction of another street.
  • A contested cell goes to the nearest street, with ties going to the older one.
  • Lots are packed by dynamic programming: 1×1 up to 4×4, with a seed stable by position.
  • A new road over cells or lots clears whatever ends up under the asphalt.

Measured on 5 scenes: free frontage coverage from 0.85–0.95 to 1.00; largest gap from 4.0 tiles to 0

Lots that grow: from 1×1 to 4×4

When a building levels up and its neighbours in the same block are free or lower, the lot merges into a bigger one. Construction restarts from zero, and ids stay stable.

Landmarks: the sumptuous ones

They appear by a deterministic roll (world seed, day and lot), with a 45 day cooldown and at most one per 750 residents. They rise through the construction stages, announce themselves through the bell and are never demolished by the game.

People and vehicles

Who moves through the city

Each vehicle is one geometry with separate surfaces: paint, glass, tyres and lights. Only the paint changes colour; headlights and tail lights come on at night and cast a pool of light on the road.

Traffic and signals

Right of way to the one on the right

Cars drive on lanes inside junctions, follow the car ahead with the IDM model and stop at the line when they lose right of way. Where there is a signal, the phases adapt to the queue.

flowchart TD
  A[Vehicle reaches the junction] --> B{Is there a signal?}
  B -- yes --> C{Green for this arm?}
  C -- no --> D[Stops at the stop line]
  C -- yes --> E{Box free of conflicting movement?}
  E -- no --> D
  E -- yes --> G[Enters and follows the connector]
  B -- no --> F{Does a conflicting movement have priority?}
  F -- yes --> D
  F -- no --> H[Reserves the box and enters]
  H --> G
  G --> I[Leaves the junction and releases the reservation]

No signal

  • The main road beats the minor one (type and width).
  • Between equals, yield to the right.
  • Whoever turns across oncoming traffic yields.
  • Reservations in arrival order, with no deadlock.

With a signal

  • Phases grouped by angle: opposite pairs together.
  • Green 3 to 8 s by queue and wait, yellow 2 s, all red 1 s.
  • A per-node offset: neighbours do not sync.
  • Conflicts in the box hold the car even on green.

Car following

  • Acceleration 1.15; braking 1.34; standstill gap 0.18 tile.
  • A 0.58 s headway and a curvature speed limit.
  • A red light becomes a stopped virtual leader.
  • Measured: 1657 trips in 600 s at 20 spawns/s, no gridlock.
Day and night

The same city, two lights

The sky is an HDRI that fades with the day: at night a readable moonlight remains, lamps get warm pools and every window turns on at its own time.

Living city

People, jobs and construction

Each lot has residents by age group, workers and a construction with its own stages. The city adds all of it into statistics that affect demand and happiness.

Construction states

stateDiagram-v2
  [*] --> Planned
  Planned --> Excavation
  Excavation --> Structure
  Structure --> Finishing
  Finishing --> Complete
  Excavation --> Paused: missing service or money
  Structure --> Paused
  Paused --> Excavation: service returns
  Complete --> Upgrading: level rises
  Upgrading --> Structure

What the city measures

  • Population by age group: children (0–14), adults (15–64) and seniors (65+).
  • Labour force, employed, unemployed and the employment rate.
  • Jobs available against filled, and the dependency ratio.
  • Workers by education; education unlocks level 3.
  • Each building shows in the inspector: construction state and percentage, residents by age and workers.

Households and ages are composed deterministically by zone and level

How we build

Design, measure, look

Geometry that shows on screen is not finished when tests pass. It follows a documented design, has invariants measured by browserless tests and is checked in the running game, day and night, including the hard cases.

  1. DesignEach area has a document in docs/process with numeric rules and invariants before the code.
  2. ResearchReferences from Cities: Skylines, TM:PE, CSUR and the fable-cities project (MIT), with licences checked.
  3. AgentsTasks in isolated copies of the repository: Codex, Muse and Opus implement; Haiku updates documents; a separate adversarial review.
  4. MeasureBrowserless tests measure overlap, gaps, sliver triangles, tangency and determinism.
  5. LookA check in the running game: day, night, game zoom and close up, and the hard cases.

Model production line

flowchart LR
  A[Python recipes] --> B[Blender in batch]
  B --> C[GLB per variant: base, x2, x4, landmark]
  C --> D[Manifest and budget gate]
  D --> E[Tests: tile, base on ground, triangles]
  E --> F[Instances in the game]

Budget: 1400 triangles per building, 2800 for 4×4; previews from two angles

Refusal list

  • Blobs, floating decals, open caps and stray arcs.
  • Notches at acute angles and z-fighting.
  • Loose kerb strips and zone cells detached from the road.
  • Any change that merely compiles.
